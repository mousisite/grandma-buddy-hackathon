Grandma Buddy Open Buddy

Someone kind to talk to, every day.

Grandma Buddy is a voice companion for an older adult who lives alone. She just talks. Buddy listens, reminds her about her pills, writes down how she feels for her doctor, and sends you a short note about her day.

It never gives medical advice. It records, reminds, and connects her to real people: you, her doctor, and emergency services.

What Buddy does

One big button to talk. Everything else, she can also just say: “Buddy, where are my glasses?”, “Read this letter to me”, “What’s the weather?”.

  • Talks with her, hands-free

    She taps once and just talks. Her words appear as she says them; Buddy answers in a warm voice and big text, then listens again. It never listens while it is talking itself.

  • A safety net, before any AI

    Plain rules check every word she says first. A warning sign like chest pain, a pill mix-up, or words of despair shows a fixed screen with big Call 911, Poison Control, or 988 buttons. It works without the internet.

  • Pill reminders

    You type each label exactly as it is on the bottle, or photograph the bottle and check what Buddy read. At each pill’s time Buddy says her name and reads the label word for word, with “I took it” and “Remind me in 10 minutes”.

  • A daily check-in and a diary

    Buddy asks how she feels and whether anything hurts, and writes it down in her own words, never its own. One page of doctor notes prints for her next visit.

  • A short update for you

    Her mood, her pills, what she noted, and any safety screen, in plain words written by the app, not the AI. It opens in her email, and nothing is sent until she presses Send.

  • Check a message

    A strange text, email, or letter? She pastes it, reads it out loud, or takes a photo. Buddy names the warning signs of a scam, looks up any phone number or website in it on the web, and always says: don’t click, don’t pay, call someone you know.

  • Read it to me

    A letter, a bill, or a form she can’t read well. She takes a photo, and Buddy says what it is and what it says, or reads it word for word. A letter about her health is only read to her, never explained.

  • A memory book

    She tells Buddy a story from her life. It becomes a page in her own words, with a painting, to send to the family or print.

  • A message, and a postcard a day

    “Tell Maria the tulips came up.” She says it, Buddy reads it back, and one tap puts it on your family page. Once a day, one cheerful thing she said becomes a painted postcard, in her own words, for her to send you.

  • Find my things

    Glasses, keys, the remote. She takes a photo of the room, and Buddy circles the thing with a soft glow and tells her where it is.

  • Today’s weather and news

    “What’s the weather?” or “Anything happening in town?” Buddy looks it up on the web near her town and says where it read it. Never anything about health.

Built on Meta’s models

Four models through Meta’s Model API, each doing the job it is best at. Plain rules check her words before any of them, and Buddy’s words after.

  • Muse Spark 1.3

    Thinks and talks: Buddy’s words, the check-in, scam checks, doctor-note topics, memory book pages, what a letter says, where her things are, and today’s weather with Meta’s web search.

  • Muse Voice Transcribe 1.0

    Hears her: live, over Meta’s realtime speech-to-text, which also hears when she has finished talking, so she never taps between turns.

  • Muse Image 1.0

    Paints one soft watercolor picture for each memory book page.

  • SAM 3.1

    Finds her glasses or keys in a photo of the room, so Buddy can circle them.

The promise

It never gives medical advice

Not what a symptom means, not what to take, not what to eat. If she asks, Buddy says kindly that her doctor is the right person, and offers to call them or write the question down. A second set of plain rules checks every reply before she hears it.

Real people come first

Emergency screens use fixed words and big call buttons, chosen by plain rules, never by the AI. When she is lonely, Buddy suggests calling you. It says it is an AI helper when asked, and never pretends otherwise.

Her data stays with her

Her diary, pills, and memory book live in a file on her own computer. Meta’s standard models answer her, and don’t learn from what she says. Photos she shows Buddy are never saved. You see only the update she chooses to send.

Questions

Is Buddy a doctor, or a nurse?

No. Buddy is an AI helper. It writes down how she feels, in her words, so her real doctor has a clear picture at the next visit. It never says what something means or what to do about it.

What happens in an emergency?

If she says something like “my chest feels tight”, a red screen with a big Call 911 button appears at once, before any AI is asked. Buddy never calls by itself: she decides. The same goes for a pill mix-up (Poison Control) and for words of despair (the 988 Lifeline, in calm words).

What does it cost to run?

The app is free and open source. It talks to Meta’s models with your own API key, and Meta bills you for what she uses: a long chat costs a few cents, an hour of hands-free listening about 18 cents, and a memory book painting a cent.

Does it need the internet?

Talking with Buddy does. The safety net and the pill reminders work on her computer without it.

Who is it for?

An older adult who lives alone and would rather talk than type, and the family who look out for her. Big words, big buttons, everything read aloud, and a Text size setting with three sizes.

Can I see what Buddy remembers about her?

Yes. Settings has “What Buddy has learned”: the people, pets, and plans she mentioned, and how she likes to talk. Any of it can be forgotten with one tap. Nothing there is about her health.

How does the family page work?

In her Settings, under “Family accounts”, make an invite code and choose what she shares: her mood, her pills, her diary in her own words, and safety screens (shown only as a kind and a time, never her words). You join at /family with the code, a name, an email, and a password, and see her day at a glance, with a note when something needs a look. Her messages and the day’s postcard land there too, when she sends them.
